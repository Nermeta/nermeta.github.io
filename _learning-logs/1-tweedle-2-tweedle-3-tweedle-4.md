## What I Found Interesting
When I started reading this book I knew that there where going to be things I did and didn't understand. what I didn't expect was for this to start from the first chapter. While the concepts are, as the book implies, simply elementary. The application of the concepts are far more reaching then Elementary would lead you to believe.

For instance, the fact that the same arithmetic rules apply to binary just as much as they do the regular counting system. Or that other languages use other bases when they are speaking to each other. Or even that you can use place value and exponents to express very large and small numbers in other base systems. That doesn't even mention that there are in fact other number systems. They are not nearly as popular, as such they aren't discussed often, but they are there. They exist!

What a wide a varied world we live in.

## What I struggled with
The biggest struggle in this chapter for me was deciphering what it was the author was trying to say. The concepts are ones that I am familiar with but the terminology lost me at times. I found it very useful to not focus so much on the individual words, but the section as a whole. As I did this it became much easier to parse what he was saying.

The other large struggle was not one that was focused on in the text but was mentioned throughout and I think it's where my understanding starts to get shaky. That is the use of Arithmetic in other bases. It isn't often that I deal with other bases at least not in such a formal way. I have done some basics in Binary back when I took classes on Circuit Design, and Hexadecimal pops up often when dealing with logs on computers. However in the case of hexadecimal most of the time you can click a button in a program and it will convert it to ASKII or whatever else you want for you. Which is fantastic don't get me wrong, but that means you don't know the mechanism behind it. 

On that note, I had a professor say one time that the difference between a designer and an engineer is that an engineer knows how the trick is done. While I didn't end up going to school for engineering I think the concept stands. You need to know how the trick is done before you can trust something to do it for you. That is how you can solve complex problems. The same mechanism or concept might pop up in unexpected places.
