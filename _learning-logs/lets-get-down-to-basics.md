## What I Achieved
I did end up finding a free version of the book utilizing one of the best internet projects on the planet the [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/understandingnum0000wuho). Which is a fantastic resource for finding source material and textbooks. They are not likely to be the newest versions but in my experience the difference between versions of textbooks only really matter in a classroom setting. As each version may put new problems in or put chapters in a slightly different order. For independent study however such changes don't matter all that much. As such you can use an older version and not have to pay an arm and a leg for it.



## What I Found Interesting
While I did not get Anki Setup as I decided to wait until after I read the books. I did begin to read *Understanding numbers in elementary school mathematics*. The writing style is not for the faint of heart. While comprehensible with an advanced vocabulary it is very precise with its wording. As such you need to have a precise understanding of the words he is using. It reads closer to a scientific paper then any textbook I have read so far. That may have more to do with my field then anything else.

That is not to say it is confusing. So far I have found his explanations incredibly well reasoned and I have never heard of the Hindu–Arabic numeral system. I have always heard it referred to as base 10 as there are 10 symbols 0-9. I also had forgotten what counting actually meant in this context. It is how many "steps" away from zero you are. I am reminded of the number line exercises I would do as a child. While this doesn't change anything in my understanding it is an interesting little nuance to keep in mind that may pay off later.
